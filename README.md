- Fresh This Week: What Changed for Coin Mixing in October 2026

Before the ranking, a quick catch-up. Three things happened over the last few weeks that matter if you’re choosing a BTC mixer today, and all of it is current as of October 29.


FinCEN withdraws its 2023 mixing rule
On October 5 the Treasury's anti-money-laundering unit said it was pulling the Section 311 proposal that would have labelled international crypto mixing a primary money-laundering concern. The withdrawal was published the next day. FinCEN's own reasoning: the definition of mixing was so wide it could chill lawful activity and bury banks in reports. It also said it will keep watching for abuse.


Self-hosted wallet reporting is off the table
The same notice killed the 2020 proposal that would have made banks and money transmitters log larger transfers to self-custody wallets and verify who owns them. It never took effect, but it hung over every withdrawal to a personal wallet for six years. Mixing into your own wallet no longer runs into that shadow.


CoinJoin is back above its 2024 level
Wasabi Wallet 2.8.3 shipped in mid-September and runs fine without zkSNACKs, the coordinator that shut down in June 2024. A 2026 Masaryk University study published in PoPETs found Wasabi CoinJoin activity has climbed past where it was before that shutdown. Non-custodial mixing is healthier today than it looked a year ago.
